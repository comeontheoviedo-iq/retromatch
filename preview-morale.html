The squad

Form and morale are stored on each man and brought up to date once a week. The chips show a word only, never a decimal. The same words appear on the line-up.

Club captain

Named eleven

    Form guide

    A rolling run of match marks, out of 10, in the manner of Match magazine's Match Facts (from 1979, every player in all four divisions) and of Football Monthly's Formscope (a mark and a short word). The numbers below are a worked example of that scale, not published marks for these matches.

    How form would be read

    Last five marks, then a word. 9 is rare, 6 to 8 is the ordinary run, 5 is a poor afternoon. The word on the chip is taken from that run. A man who has not played has no marks yet and shows Fair until he does.

    The dressing room

    After the match

    The full-time talk already in the 1980s match day, and a proposed nudge to each man's morale as well as the existing next-week form multiplier (held between 0.96 and 1.04).

    What moves morale

    Updated weekly, in small steps, for your men and for every other club's men under the same rules.

    • The result, for those who played
    • Being dropped after a run in the side, or recalled to it
    • Games played against a man's place in the side (a regular left out is not the same as a squad man waiting)
    • The wage row, and whether he is on the list or retained
    • The captaincy, given or taken away
    • The half-time and full-time talks, including the existing next-week multiplier
    • A word of praise or criticism in the papers

    The papers

    Praise and criticism travel through the back pages and the local press, and through the dressing room, not through modern media. The report below uses only the sourced result. The manager's choices are a proposed sheet, not a real quotation.
