RetroMatch: era commentary preview

PREVIEW This is not in the game. Four full matches, each taken from a real run of the game's match simulation on a real save, then told in the voice of the period. Every line on the left is chosen from what the sim's event log (right) says happened: the same scorers, the same minutes, the same saves, misses and corners. Nothing is invented or re-rolled. The style is evoked, not quoted from any real commentator.

New in this version: for 1946 and 1950 the running commentary is now live, in the present tense (“Stubbins is through... he shoots...”), and at full time a short past-tense newspaper report of the same match follows. 1959 and 1966-67 are unchanged.

Era rules respected: charging the keeper still lawful in 1946; floodlights only for a real midweek fixture after Feb 1956; one injury-only substitute in 1966-67; no yellow or red cards before 1970 (the referee only takes a name).

Saturday 24 August

ARSENAL BEAT LIVERPOOL

Arsenal 4, Liverpool 2. Arsenal: Wade 24, 54, Compton 61, Scott 73 (pen). Liverpool: Done 45, Liddell 46.

A crowd of 42,769 saw Arsenal beat Liverpool by four goals to two at Highbury on Saturday in a First Division match. The setting was a blustery, showery afternoon, with the pitch firm, true surface, and Mr. S. Rous was the referee.

The first half brought two goals. J. Wade finished a move begun by A. Fields in the twenty-fourth minute, which put Arsenal ahead; then C. Done scored on the stroke of half-time, after A. Stubbins had made the opening, which brought Liverpool level, 1-1. It was 1-1 at half-time.

After the interval there were four more. B. Liddell hit a hard shot from the edge of the area past the goalkeeper just after the interval, and Liverpool were in front, 1-2; Wade scored from a corner in the fifty-fourth minute, his second of the afternoon, and the scores were level at 2-2; L. Compton scored in the sixty-first minute, after Wade had made the opening, and Arsenal were in front, 3-2; then L. Scott converted the penalty-kick in the seventy-third minute, making it 4-2. Nothing more was added, and the match ended 4-2.

Among the other incidents, P. Sloan of Arsenal went off hurt in the opening minutes, and Arsenal played on a man short, there being no substitutes; the referee took the name of W. Fagan of Liverpool for a heavy tackle; there was a fine save by Platt, in the Arsenal goal.

Arsenal had six shots at goal to Liverpool's four.

The best man on the field, in the view of the press box, was Wade, of Arsenal.
